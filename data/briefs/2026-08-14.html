Today's lesson

Concept

Factoring in Trade Finance

Factoring is a financial mechanism where a business sells its accounts receivable (invoices for goods delivered) to a specialized financial institution called a factor. The factor advances most of the cash upfront, typically 80% to 90% of the invoice value, and collects the full amount from the buyer later. The factor deducts a service fee and remits the balance.

In Bangladesh, trade has historically relied on Letters of Credit (LCs), which require substantial bank collateral and extensive documentation. By permitting factoring and digital trade documents, Bangladesh Bank allows suppliers to unlock working capital quickly without waiting months for customer payments.
