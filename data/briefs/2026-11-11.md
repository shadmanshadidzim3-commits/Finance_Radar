## The day in one read

Today was defined by the structural collision between external payment liabilities and administrative stopgaps. The single dominant force was the widening strain on commercial foreign exchange liquidity, as elevated fuel imports pushed letter of credit settlement rates above Tk 125 per dollar. Rather than outright dollar sales, Bangladesh Bank responded by deferring foreign currency obligations, granting 90-day deferred payment terms for essential food imports.

What changed versus recent sessions is the explicit shift from settling past dues to postponing future ones. **1. Commercial dollar rates crossed Tk 125 as surging fuel import bills pushed letter of credit settlements to Tk 124.97, according to The Business Standard.**

A letter of credit (LC) is a bank guarantee ensuring an exporter receives payment once goods are shipped. Rising fuel import liabilities are forcing commercial banks to bid aggressively for greenbacks in the interbank market, depleting net open positions and putting structural depreciation pressure on the taka.

**2. Bangladesh Bank introduced a 90-day deferred payment facility for essential Ramadan food imports through mid-March 2027 to prevent price surges.**

Deferred payment terms function as trade credit, where buyers take delivery now and settle foreign exchange obligations up to 90 days later. This eases spot foreign exchange demand in the short term, but it stacks aggregate foreign currency liabilities into early 2027.

A fiscal deficit occurs when government expenditures exceed tax revenues, forcing the state to borrow from the banking sector or issue treasury bills. With revenue reaching under Tk 1.28 lakh crore against a Tk 1.78 lakh crore target, government bank borrowing risks crowding out private credit and sustaining high yields.
