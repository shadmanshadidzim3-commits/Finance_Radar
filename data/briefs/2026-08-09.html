Today's lesson

Concept

Sovereign Credit Outlook and Foreign Borrowing Channels

A sovereign credit rating measures a nation's ability to service foreign debt. Rating agencies assess fiscal deficits, foreign exchange reserves, and macroeconomic stability. When an agency changes an outlook from stable to negative, it signals a high risk of an imminent credit downgrade.

For Bangladeshi banks, a sovereign outlook downgrade immediately increases the cost of issuing trade letters of credit (LCs). Foreign correspondent banks view local bank obligations as higher risk. They charge elevated LC confirmation fees and demand higher interest rates on short-term dollar lines.

This raises the foreign currency import bill for local businesses, narrows bank net interest margins, and forces faster domestic currency depreciation.
