Today's lesson

Concept

Fiscal crowding out

When a government runs a budget deficit, it must finance the shortfall by borrowing from the central bank, foreign lenders, or domestic commercial banks. If it borrows heavily from commercial banks, as seen with Bangladesh's Tk 16,601 crore intake, it competes directly with private businesses for the fixed pool of bank deposits. Because commercial banks consider sovereign treasury bills virtually risk-free, they prefer lending to the state rather than extending loans to private industrial enterprises. This dynamic reduces the supply of loanable funds available to private companies, drives up borrowing costs, and dampens private capital expenditure.
