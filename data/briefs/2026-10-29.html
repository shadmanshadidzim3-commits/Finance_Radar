Concept

The Crowding Out Effect

When the government absorbs tens of thousands of crores to fund civil service payrolls and energy liabilities, banks have less capital remaining for commercial lending. Because credit becomes scarce, banks raise lending rates. Private entrepreneurs find borrowing either unaffordable or completely unavailable. Consequently, private factory expansions, capital expenditures, and job creation are 'crowded out' by the sovereign's financing requirements.

Non-performing loan (NPL)

A loan where the borrower has stopped making scheduled interest or principal payments for an extended duration, usually 90 days or more.

Speakers at a Dhaka banking conference revealed that 80% of the financial sector's non-performing loans are deliberate defaults.

An NPL is dead capital on a bank balance sheet that consumes loan loss provisions rather than earning interest.

Fiscal deficit

The financial gap that occurs when government spending exceeds total tax revenues and fees collected over a period.

The National Board of Revenue recorded an alarming Tk 43,000 crore revenue shortfall, dramatically widening the fiscal deficit.

It is the government's budget overdraft, financed either through borrowing from commercial banks or issuing treasury debt.

Crowding out

A dynamic where heavy government borrowing consumes available bank funds, leaving less credit and higher loan rates for private businesses.

With tax collections missing targets, escalated sovereign borrowing from domestic banks threatens to crowd out private industrial borrowers.

When the government takes the front seat at the credit counter, private enterprises are pushed out onto the pavement.

Cost-push inflation

A broad increase in consumer prices driven by rising costs of raw materials, energy, and labor rather than excess consumer demand.

Proposed LPG price hikes alongside garment worker demands for a Tk 30,000 minimum wage threaten to inject cost-push inflation across the economy.

Cost-push inflation means goods cost more to manufacture, so they inevitably cost more to buy.

Pricing power

The competitive ability of a business to raise its selling prices without losing customer volume or market share.

Square Pharmaceuticals posted a record annual net profit of Tk 2,770 crore because its market leadership gives it strong pricing power against imported input costs.

If a company can increase prices by 10% and customers keep lining up, it possesses genuine pricing power.
