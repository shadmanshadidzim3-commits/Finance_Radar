Today's lesson

Concept

Cost-Push Inflation

Inflation is usually caused by one of two things: people having too much money to spend (demand-pull) or the cost of making things going up (cost-push). Today, Bangladesh is experiencing classic cost-push inflation. It starts with Brent crude oil prices rising globally.

Because oil is used for transport and power, every business in Bangladesh suddenly finds its costs rising. To stay profitable, they raise their prices. Then, workers find they can't afford food, so they demand higher wages.

If those wages are granted, businesses raise prices again to cover the new wage bill. This creates a 'spiral' where prices keep rising not because people are rich, but because production is getting more expensive.

Test yourselfIf the price of rice rises because a flood destroyed the crops, is that demand-pull or cost-push inflation? (Answer: Cost-push, because the supply decreased and the cost to acquire rice increased.)

Jargon decoder

Margin compression

When a company's costs go up but its selling prices stay the same, leaving it with less profit.

Garment factories face this as workers demand higher wages while global buyers resist price increases.

Think of it as being squeezed between a rising floor (costs) and a fixed ceiling (prices).

Fiscal consolidation

Policies aimed at reducing government deficits and debt accumulation.

The NBR's plan for a 15% VAT rate is a classic example of trying to fix the national budget.

It is the government's version of going on a strict financial diet.

Credit risk

The danger that someone you lent money to (or deposited money with) won't pay you back.

Desco is facing this because Tk 114 crore of its cash is stuck in 'distressed' banks.

It is the 'will I get my money back?' factor in every transaction.

Operational leverage

A measure of how much a company can increase its profit by increasing its revenue.

Renata used this to grow profits by 64% through better management even as the economy struggled.

High leverage means a small increase in sales leads to a giant jump in profit.
