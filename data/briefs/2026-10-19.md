**1. Government spent $4.12 billion on energy subsidies over the past eight months.**

According to The Daily Star, Prime Minister's adviser Rashed Al Mahmud Titumir confirmed the outlay, driven by elevated international oil and LNG rates. This expenditure expands the fiscal deficit — the shortfall when government spending exceeds revenue — and directly depletes public resources available for development. **4. Fuel import letters of credit jumped approximately 63% year-on-year in the first quarter.**

Reported by state news agency BSS, the value of fuel import letters of credit — financial contracts issued by banks guaranteeing payment to overseas exporters upon cargo delivery — expanded significantly. This surge reflects persistent energy import costs that absorb dollar liquidity from commercial bank nostro accounts, which are foreign currency accounts held abroad. If import commitments expand faster than export earnings, commercial banks face persistent dollar liquidity deficits.

**5. BSEC decides fate of 12 subsidiaries tied to nine failing non-bank financial institutions.**

According to The Daily Star, BSEC intervened in capital market subsidiaries belonging to troubled non-bank financial institutions (NBFIs) — financial intermediaries that offer loans and investment facilities but cannot take demand deposits. The regulator aims to ring-fence operational assets from being liquidated to cover parent-company default liabilities. This separation protects retail trading accounts and prevents systemic contagion across brokerage houses.

**6. BSEC proposes a 50% debt repayment cap and mandatory escrow accounts for IPO proceeds.**

According to The Business Standard, BSEC's draft rules prohibit issuers from using equity listing proceeds to clear defaulted or rescheduled loans. Companies must deposit funds into an escrow account — a legally restricted holding account where cash cannot be released until independent auditors certify regulatory compliance. This closes a loophole where promoters issued shares merely to shift private credit defaults onto retail public equity investors.
