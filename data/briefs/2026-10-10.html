Today's lesson

Concept

Sovereign-Bank Doom Loop

When commercial banks accumulate massive bad loans, they become insolvent and require government bailouts. To rescue them, the government issues sovereign debt. Local banks buy this debt using their remaining deposits, becoming heavily invested in government bonds.

If the government's budget deficit deepens and its credit weakens, the market value of those bonds falls, inflicting fresh losses on the banks. The banks then need another bailout, trapping the sovereign and the financial sector in an interlocking downward spiral. Today's Tk 60,000 crore annual bank repair projection, paired with rejecting external IMF loans in favour of local borrowing, illustrates this precise mechanism.
