Today's lesson

Concept

The Impossible Trinity

The Impossible Trinity (or Trilemma) states that a country cannot have all three at once: a fixed exchange rate, free capital movement, and an independent monetary policy. Bangladesh is currently struggling with this. To control inflation (independent policy), the central bank wants to keep interest rates high.

However, to support the economy, it also tries to manage the Taka's value (fixed/managed exchange rate). As dollars become scarce, the central bank must either let the Taka fall (giving up the fixed rate) or burn through reserves to defend it. Today's news of the Taka hitting Tk 125.73 shows the 'managed' rate is breaking under the pressure of global market forces.

Test yourselfIf Bangladesh Bank wants to keep the Taka at exactly Tk 120 while US interest rates are rising, what must it do to its own interest rates or reserves? (Answer: It must either raise interest rates to attract dollars or sell its dollar reserves to buy Taka.)

Jargon decoder

These terms appear in today's brief. Hover any dotted word above to see its meaning without scrolling back.

Deferred payment

An agreement to pay for goods at a later date rather than immediately upon delivery.

Bangladesh Bank is using this to delay the demand for dollars for food imports.

It is like a credit card for the country; it helps today but must be paid back with interest or at future exchange rates.

Authorized capital

The maximum amount of share capital that a company is legally allowed to issue to shareholders.

Islami Bank is raising this limit so it can potentially issue more shares to cover its massive losses.

Think of it as the size of the bucket; raising it doesn't put money in the bucket, but it allows for more money to be poured in later.

Provision shortfall

When a bank has not set aside enough money to cover the expected losses from loans that might not be paid back.

Islami Bank's shortfall of over Tk 85,000 crore shows the extreme stress on its balance sheet.

It is the gap between the 'rainy day fund' you have and the 'rainy day fund' the law says you need.

Non-performing loan (NPL)

A loan where the borrower has stopped making scheduled payments for a specific period, usually 90 days.

The debt crisis at AB Apparels is a high-profile example of the NPLs weighing down the banking system.

An NPL is a 'dead' asset that stops earning interest for the bank and starts eating its capital.
