## Top 10 stories

**1. Crude oil jumped above $91 per barrel following US-Iran clashes around the Strait of Hormuz.**

Higher oil prices directly inflate Bangladesh's import bill, draining foreign exchange reserves — the central bank's stock of foreign currency used to settle international trade. This widens the current account deficit, which measures the gap between foreign currency inflows and outflows from trade and transfers. It also forces the government to choose between widening the fiscal deficit or passing price hikes to consumers.

**2. Government net bank borrowing reached Tk 165,538 crore in FY26, overshooting its target by Tk 47,500 crore.**

Excessive sovereign borrowing causes bank disintermediation and credit crowding-out — where public debt absorbs loanable bank funds, leaving less credit for private businesses. It forces Bangladesh Bank to manage liquidity strain across commercial lenders through repo facilities, the short-term central bank lending window that provides cash against government securities.

**3. Bangladesh secures a slight effective tariff advantage over China and Vietnam in US apparel exports.**

Garment manufacturing generates over 80% of Bangladesh's export earnings. An effective tariff margin against competing exporters protects volume demand in the United States, supporting the trade balance — the net difference between export receipts and import payments. However, rising cotton input costs could quickly erode this nominal tariff edge.

**4. The government will sign a $1.0 billion IsDB loan agreement on September 3 for the Eastern Refinery unit 2 expansion.**

Domestic crude refining capacity substitutes expensive refined fuel imports with cheaper unrefined crude. This long-term capital expenditure project will ease future balance of payments pressure. Concessional external financing from multilateral lenders also supplies foreign exchange without depleting domestic banking liquidity.

**5. The NBR exempted firms with annual turnover up to Tk 2 crore from the minimum turnover tax.**

Minimum turnover tax is an advance levy calculated as a percentage of gross sales regardless of net profit. Eliminating it for small enterprises relieves operating cash flow stress. However, it slightly narrows the National Board of Revenue's direct tax base during a period of acute fiscal deficit pressure.

Holding domestic retail fuel prices steady shields transport and industrial consumers from immediate imported inflation. However, with Brent crude hovering near $89, freezing pump prices increases the operating losses of state-owned Bangladesh Petroleum Corporation. This creates an unbudgeted energy subsidy liability for the treasury.
