Today's lesson

Concept

Administered pricing lag

In a free market, retail prices change immediately when underlying commodity costs shift. Administered pricing occurs when a state regulator—such as the Bangladesh Energy Regulatory Commission—sets consumer prices by official order. Because regulatory bodies meet periodically and review historical costs, their price changes always lag real-time global markets.

Today, global Brent crude dropped 11.07%, but retail LPG cylinder prices in Bangladesh rose by Tk 223 to Tk 2,060. The regulator raised prices based on expensive fuel contracts signed weeks ago. This lag means consumers and factories often suffer peak utility costs even as international market pressures are actively collapsing.

Operating margin

The percentage of revenue a business keeps after paying for day-to-day production costs like wages and fabric.

Current account deficit

The shortfall when a country spends more foreign currency on imported goods and services than it earns from its exports.

A plunge in Brent crude directly narrows our current account deficit by lowering the foreign currency needed for fuel.

Think of it as a country's national trade overdraft.

Capital expenditure

Funds that a company spends to buy physical equipment, machinery, or factories to grow its business.
