## Top 10 stories

**1. The National Board of Revenue logged a revenue shortfall exceeding Tk 50,000 crore in the first four months of FY27.**

Tax collection reached less than Tk 1.28 lakh crore against a Tk 1.78 lakh crore target, according to preliminary NBR estimates reported by The Business Standard. This fiscal deficit — the shortfall when government spending outpaces its revenues — forces the state to borrow from commercial banks. High sovereign borrowing crowds out private sector credit and keeps domestic borrowing rates elevated.

**2. Listed commercial banks posted an aggregate net loss of Tk 1,424 crore in the third quarter as nine lenders bleed.**

The losses stem from loan-loss provisioning — capital banks must set aside by law to absorb expected losses from non-performing loans. As non-performing loans rise, provisioning eats directly into pre-tax earnings. This capital erosion restricts the banking sector's ability to issue new credit to productive industries.

**3. The US 10-year Treasury yield crossed 5.0% following stronger-than-expected US October consumer inflation data.**

The US 10-year yield serves as the global risk-free rate — the baseline return investors demand before taking risks elsewhere. When US yields rise, emerging market capital outflows accelerate as foreign portfolio managers seek safer dollar yields. This strengthens the US dollar index and intensifies taka depreciation pressure.

**4. The Investment Corporation of Bangladesh faces systemic stress as the Tk 3,000 crore Beximco Green Sukuk approaches maturity.** `ICB BEXIMCO`

A sukuk is an Islamic financial certificate similar to a bond. Exposure is spread across 22 commercial banks and subsidiaries. If repayment falters, classified investments — financial assets marked as impaired or defaulted — will surge across the banking sector.
