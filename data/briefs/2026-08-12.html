Today's lesson

Concept

Price-to-Book (P/B) Ratio in Margin Lending Rules

The Price-to-Book (P/B) ratio divides a company's stock price per share by its net accounting asset value per share. A high P/B means the market prices the company well above its physical assets. Regulators often set rules restricting investors from using borrowed money—margin loans—to buy stocks with very high or low P/B ratios.

When BSEC scraps the P/B rule for margin lending, investors gain freedom to use leverage on a wider range of stocks regardless of accounting asset backing. This increases overall market trading volume quickly, but raises leverage risk if stock prices unexpectedly drop.
