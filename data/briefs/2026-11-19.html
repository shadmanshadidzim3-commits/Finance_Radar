Today's lesson

Concept

The Impossible Trinity (Mundell-Fleming Trilemma)

The Impossible Trinity is a core macroeconomic principle stating that a country can only choose two of the following three policy goals: a fixed or managed exchange rate, free movement of capital, and an independent monetary policy. If a central bank wants to manage its exchange rate—like Bangladesh Bank keeping the taka stable against the dollar—and allow capital to flow across borders, it must surrender control over domestic interest rates. When capital outflows occur and foreign reserves dwindle, defending the currency requires heavy dollar sales, which contracts domestic money supply and forces interest rates higher. Today's news illustrates this trilemma in real time: Bangladesh Bank is burning through reserves to defend the taka while trying to manage severe domestic banking sector liquidity.
