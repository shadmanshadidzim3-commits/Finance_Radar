The central bank allowed taka depreciation — a reduction in the official value of local currency against foreign money — after its administrative cap failed to curb demand. **2. Private sector credit growth plummeted to a 33-year low as domestic businesses halted capital expenditure plans.**

Private credit growth measures the rate at which commercial banks expand loans to private companies. When this growth stalls, capital expenditure — spending on physical assets like machinery and factories — stops, signaling deep risk aversion among corporate borrowers. Without new investment, future economic growth slows and bank earnings from lending fees shrink.

**3. The NBR missed its annual tax collection target by Tk 88,000 crore, completing a 10-year streak of fiscal shortfalls.**

The tax revenue shortfall widens the country's fiscal deficit — the gap where government spending exceeds its revenue collections. To cover this gap, the Treasury must issue more government bonds, which leads to fiscal crowding out as banks lend to the government instead of private companies. It also leaves less room for essential infrastructure spending.

**4. Islami Bank Bangladesh reported a massive net loss of Tk 1,028 crore for the April-June quarter.** `ISLAMIBANK`

The loss stems from provision requirements — funds a bank must set aside by law to cover expected losses from non-performing loans (NPLs), which are loans where borrowers have stopped making timely payments. These write-downs erode the bank's regulatory capital adequacy ratio, reducing its legal capacity to issue fresh loans and depressing overall banking sector valuations.

**5. Commercial banks lowered deposit rates further below inflation following Bangladesh Bank's policy rate cut.**

When interest rates fall below inflation, the country experiences negative real interest rates — a scenario where inflation erodes the real value of cash faster than interest accrues. This shrinks net interest margin, which is the spread between what banks earn on loans and pay on deposits. Savers are incentivised to pull cash out of formal bank deposits and move into tangible assets or informal channels.
