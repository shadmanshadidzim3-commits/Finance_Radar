Bangladesh Bank allowed the currency to devalue to Tk 123.47 to reflect market realities. **2. Private sector credit growth plummeted to a 33-year low as businesses halted capital investment.**

Weak private sector credit growth — the rate at which commercial bank lending to private businesses expands — signals a halt in corporate capital expenditure (CapEx). Without borrowing for factories and machinery, future economic growth slows down and job creation stalls.

**3. The NBR missed its annual tax collection target by Tk 88,000 crore for the tenth consecutive year.**

The Tk 88,000 crore shortfall widens the fiscal deficit — the gap where government spending exceeds tax revenue. To cover this gap, the government must borrow heavily from commercial banks, which crowds out private sector borrowers.

<sub>BD Macro · 🇧🇩 direct · confidence: high · The Daily Star · [read](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/economy/news/nbr-misses-revenue-target-10th-straight-year-4240256)</sub>

**4. Commercial banks lowered deposit returns further below inflation following central bank rate cuts.**

Lower deposit rates push the real rate of return — the nominal interest rate minus inflation — deeper into negative territory. When real returns are negative, bank deposits lose purchasing power, discouraging domestic savings and driving capital into unproductive physical assets.

**5. Bangladesh signed a landmark CEPA trade deal with South Korea granting tariff preferences on 97% of exports.**

This preferential trade agreement — a treaty reducing import tariffs between partner nations — protects Bangladesh's export competitiveness as it prepares to lose LDC trade benefits. It gives garment and leather exporters duty-free access to a major Asian market.

<sub>RMG/Export · 🇧🇩 direct · confidence: high · The Business Standard, Prothom Alo · [read](https://www.tbsnews.net/economy/dhaka-seoul-sign-cepa-97-bangladeshi-87-s-korean-goods-get-preferential-tariff-access-1506491)</sub>

**6. Brent crude prices fell 5.42% to $79.23 per barrel, easing Bangladesh's energy import bill.**

Cheaper crude lowers imported inflation — price increases caused by rising foreign input costs. Because Bangladesh imports almost all its petroleum, falling benchmark prices reduce dollar outflows and ease energy subsidy costs for the government treasury.

**7. Severe gas shortages reduced plastic factory operations to 30% of total plant capacity.**

A drop in the capacity utilization rate — the proportion of potential manufacturing output actually produced — raises unit costs for manufacturers. Because plastic products are crucial input packaging for RMG and food industries, this bottleneck slows down broader export supply chains.

**8. Bangladesh Bank removed lending limits on five major branches of state-owned Sonali Bank.**

Lifting the credit ceiling — a regulatory limit on maximum loan volume a bank unit can issue — frees liquidity for state projects and corporate borrowers. However, doing this at a state bank carrying high non-performing loans carries credit quality risks.
