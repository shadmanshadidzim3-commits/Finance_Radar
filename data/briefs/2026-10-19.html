Today's lesson

Concept

Fiscal Dominance

Fiscal dominance occurs when a country's government budget deficit and public debt burdens become so massive that the central bank loses its ability to control inflation independently. In normal times, a central bank raises interest rates to tame rising prices. However, when fiscal dominance takes hold, higher interest rates dramatically increase the government's borrowing costs, threatening sovereign insolvency.

As seen in Bangladesh today, with the state spending $4.12 billion on fuel subsidies and borrowing heavily from domestic banks, central bank policy often ends up being subordinated to funding the treasury's cash shortfalls. Monetary policy becomes a tool to keep the government liquid rather than a tool to stabilise the currency.
