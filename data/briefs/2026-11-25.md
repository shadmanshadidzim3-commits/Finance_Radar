## The day in one read

Today was defined by a fragile resolution in the labor market overshadowed by a deepening energy and fiscal crisis. The dominant force is the RMG wage hike to Tk 17,000, which provides a necessary social floor but arrives exactly when global gas prices are spiking and power supply from India is being halved. This creates a severe cost-push environment for the nation's primary export engine.

What changed today is the quantification of the fiscal strain. The 72% jump in government bank borrowing confirms that revenue shortfalls are now directly competing with the private sector for credit. This explains why we are seeing distressed large-cap corporates like GPH Ispat attempting to convert massive bank debts into bonds—the banks simply do not have the liquidity to keep rolling over traditional loans.

What to watch next is the central bank's reaction to the Adani power cut. If Bangladesh Bank does not prioritize dollar payments for energy imports, the resulting load-shedding will cripple factory productivity just as the new, higher wages take effect. The one number to watch is the USD/BDT median; if it stays anchored below 125.00, it suggests the central bank is still using moral suasion to prevent a breakout despite these mounting pressures.
