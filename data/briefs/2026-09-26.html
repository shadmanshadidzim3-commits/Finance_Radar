Today's lesson

Concept

The Debt Service Drag

When a country borrows foreign currency, it must periodically pay back principal (the borrowed amount) and interest. The total payment is called debt servicing. In July and August, Bangladesh repaid $699 million to external creditors but received only $295 million in new external loans.

That created a negative net flow of $404 million. To settle that difference in dollars, the central bank must draw on foreign exchange reserves unless export receipts or remittances cover the gap. When debt servicing exceeds new concessional inflows, it acts like a siphon on domestic reserves, restricting the foreign currency available for essential commercial imports.

Policy repo rate

The interest rate a central bank charges commercial banks when it lends them money for short periods.

Bangladesh Bank kept this rate steady to avoid choking economic growth while checking the inflationary impact of higher fuel prices.

It is the master interest rate that sets the floor for all borrowing costs across the economy.

Fiscal deficit

The cash shortage that happens when a government spends more money in a year than it collects in taxes and fees.

Burning through 45% of annual power subsidies in three months threatens to blow this deficit wide open.

Think of it as the government's annual credit card bill that must be covered by borrowing.

Deleveraging

The deliberate process of paying off debt to make a company or government financially safer.

Apex Footwear is issuing Tk 500 crore in preference shares specifically to retire high-interest bank loans.

Trading debt for equity to reduce monthly interest bills.

Preference shares

Company shares that pay a guaranteed fixed dividend before regular shareholders get any profit, but usually have no voting rights.

Apex Footwear is using them as a cheaper financing alternative to expensive commercial bank loans.

A hybrid between a loan and a stock: fixed income safety without loan foreclosure risk.
