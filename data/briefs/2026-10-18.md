## Top 10 stories

**1. The government decided to halt purchasing electricity from private plants at rates exceeding the national average tariff, according to The Business Standard.**

The decision targets capacity charges — contractually guaranteed fees paid to power producers regardless of whether electricity is generated. Capping power purchase costs aims to curb the ballooning fiscal deficit, which is the gap when government spending exceeds tax revenues. It will squeeze margins for high-cost independent power producers while lowering the sovereign subsidy burden.

**2. Disbursements under Bangladesh Bank's Tk 20,000 crore factory revival refinance scheme expanded by Tk 450 crore in 18 days, reported The Business Standard.**

The facility provides liquidity support through a refinance line — a low-interest lending facility funded by the central bank to commercial lenders. Speeding up disbursements seeks to revive non-operational assets before they turn into permanent non-performing loans, which are loans in default where borrowers have stopped making interest payments. However, channeling credit to distressed legacy units risks adding balance-sheet strain to participating banks if factory cash flows fail to rebound.

**3. Aggregate net profit of foreign commercial banks operating in Bangladesh dropped 38% year-on-year in the first half of 2026, according to The Business Standard.**

Foreign banks depend heavily on trade finance — credit instruments like letters of credit that facilitate cross-border import and export transactions. Lower profits reflect contracting trade volumes alongside compressed net interest margin, the spread between what a bank earns on assets and pays on liabilities. When conservative multinational banks pull back on fee generation and trade volumes, foreign exchange liquidity in the interbank market contracts.

**5. BSEC ordered all listed companies to park declared cash dividends in dedicated escrow bank accounts, according to The Daily Star.**

The directive prevents working capital diversion — the practice of using declared shareholder cash to plug operating cash deficits. Segregating cash into escrow accounts ensures immediate dividend payout capability and exposes companies suffering from liquidity mismatches. While this strengthens corporate governance, highly leveraged issuers may face short-term operating liquidity crunches.

Bangladesh imports almost all its petroleum products, meaning high crude prices translate into imported inflation — rising domestic prices driven by costlier foreign goods. Costlier oil worsens the current account balance, which measures trade in goods and services plus transfers. This widens sovereign foreign currency outflows and increases upward pressure on domestic power generation costs.
