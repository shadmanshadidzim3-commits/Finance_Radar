## The day in one read

Today was characterized by the sovereign recognizing severe macroeconomic constraints while deploying emergency regulatory tools to stabilize domestic financial institutions. The IMF's downgrade of Bangladesh's FY27 growth to 3.2% and inflation forecast of 10.9% confirms that the economy is entering stagflation. The state's immediate response has been heavy reliance on domestic banks, taking Tk 24,245 crore in credit while opening long-term negotiations for $10 billion in World Bank assistance.

What changed versus recent sessions is the explicit admission that external multilateral loan disbursements will take months rather than weeks. While past briefs highlighted temporary liquidity cushions from bilateral energy credit lines, today's government approvals for nine spot LNG cargoes prove that immediate foreign currency outflows remain unavoidable. Meanwhile, the central bank is forced to backstop commercial lenders, allowing National Bank to reopen unrestricted cash withdrawals following emergency liquidity support.

What to watch next is whether commercial bank deposit withdrawals accelerate as restrictions lift on October 15, or if interbank confidence steadies. The single decisive number to monitor is the corroborated median USD/BDT exchange rate, currently at Tk 123.18. If spot dollar pricing moves past Tk 123.80, it will signal that emergency fuel import settlements are overwhelming commercial bank foreign currency buffers.
