## Top 10 stories

**1. Foreign loan commitments drop 37% to a 14-year low of $5.24 billion as annual debt servicing climbs to a record $4.49 billion.**

The country faces net foreign cash drain because foreign loan disbursements — the actual cash arriving from lenders — fell 5.8% to $8.07 billion. Meanwhile, debt servicing — the combined payment of principal and interest on foreign borrowing — consumed over half of those inflows, widening the external financing gap and keeping devaluation pressure on the taka.

**2. Polish retail giant LPP suspends garment purchases from Bangladesh over $40 million in overdue supplier payment disputes.**

The order freeze directly hits export revenue and threatens trade credit — the practice where suppliers ship goods now and receive payment later based on trust. Payment defaults damage buyer trust across the Ready-Made Garment (RMG) sector, which generates over 80% of Bangladesh's foreign currency earnings.

**3. Treasury bill yields and call money rates fall sharply after Bangladesh Bank cuts its policy rate to 9.50%.**

The transmission mechanism — the channel through which central bank rate decisions alter interest rates across the entire financial system — took effect within 24 hours. Lower Treasury bill yields reduce interest costs for government borrowing, but lower deposit yields risk pushing savers toward informal channels if inflation stays high.

**4. Brent crude plummets 7.08% to $83.74 per barrel as diplomatic talks between the US and Iran ease supply disruption fears.**

Crude oil is Bangladesh's primary energy import, directly driving the government's import bill and energy subsidy burden — the financial support the state pays to keep domestic fuel prices below world market costs. A lower import bill reduces foreign exchange drain, providing instant relief to current account pressure.

**5. Chattogram Port congestion ties up 9,330 stranded containers, eating 40% of port capacity and causing $5 billion in trade losses.**

Port congestion increases turn-around time for cargo ships and extends working capital cycles — the duration cash is tied up in raw materials and finished goods before being sold. Delayed raw material imports stall manufacturing, while delayed clothing exports create buyer penalties.

Rising US yields broaden the interest rate differential — the gap in benchmark interest rates between two currencies.
