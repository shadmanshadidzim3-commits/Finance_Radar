## The day in one read

Today was characterized by tightening liquidity across both domestic fiscal accounts and capital markets as high global energy costs hit home. The dominant macroeconomic force is the ongoing energy burden, evidenced by a 65% surge in petroleum import letter of credit commitments and Brent crude sustaining near $110.29 per barrel. This external bill is forcing the sovereign to draw heavily on domestic commercial banks, borrowing Tk 16,601 crore in two months and draining liquidity from the wider economy.

What changed versus recent sessions is the visible exhaustion of domestic capital market liquidity. While previous notes highlighted emergency liquidity guarantees stabilizing individual distressed banks, today demonstrated that broader system liquidity is drying up. DSE turnover touched a nine-month low, and commercial lenders cut deposit rates despite high inflation, pushing real returns deeper into negative territory.

What to watch next is whether commercial banks raise prime lending rates as government crowding out accelerates throughout the second quarter. The critical metric to track is net domestic credit expansion to the private sector; if private credit growth falls below 8.0%, industrial capital investment will experience severe deceleration.
