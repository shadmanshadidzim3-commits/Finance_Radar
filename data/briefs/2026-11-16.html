Today's lesson

Concept

Asset-liability mismatch

An asset-liability mismatch occurs when the financial characteristics of what an institution owns (assets) do not match what it owes (liabilities). The most common mismatches involve maturity (lending long-term while borrowing short-term deposits) and currency (earning revenues in domestic currency while owing debt in foreign currency). Today's news highlights both risks.

When commercial banks lend long-term money to risky borrowers funded by short-term customer deposits, non-performing loans freeze assets while deposit liabilities remain payable on demand. Similarly, if the government borrows via international sovereign bonds at 5.15% US Treasury yields, its liabilities are in dollars while its tax revenues are in taka. A depreciating currency makes servicing that foreign debt exponentially more expensive.

A loan where the borrower has stopped making scheduled payments for at least 90 days.

Currency swap

A contract where two parties exchange different currencies today and reverse the trade later at a set rate.

The IFC proposed lending taka locally by swapping foreign currency with Bangladesh Bank.

It allows a company to borrow in home currency while the central bank holds the foreign currency collateral.

Trade deficit

The negative financial balance that occurs when a nation imports more merchandise than it exports.

Bangladesh logged a cumulative trade deficit of $24.17 billion, driven by heavy trade gaps with India.

A trade deficit measures net physical goods leaving versus entering; it represents a net exit of cash.

Sukuk

An Islamic financial certificate that grants an investor partial ownership in a tangible asset rather than debt interest.

Bangladesh Bank announced an auction of Tk 3,000 crore in short-term Sukuk for rural infrastructure.

Conventional bonds pay interest on debt; Sukuk pay a share of profit generated by a real underlying asset.
