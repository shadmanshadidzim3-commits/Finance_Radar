## The day in one read

Today was characterized by a stark realization of Bangladesh's 'negative net inflow' problem. The dominant force was the data showing the country paid $2.04 in debt servicing for every $1 it received in loans. This is a structural drain on dollars that no amount of positive GDP headlines can mask. When you combine this with a 34% widening of the trade deficit, the pressure on the taka becomes a matter of arithmetic, not just sentiment.

What changed versus recent days is the physical manifestation of the energy crisis. While we have tracked rising fuel prices for weeks, today's grid failure—cutting 5,370MW—shows the system is hitting a breaking point. The 25-30% capacity loss reported by the RMG sector is a direct hit to the country's primary source of foreign exchange. We are moving from a price crisis to a production crisis.

What to watch next is the government's success in securing the $1 billion Korean budget support. This is no longer a luxury; it is a necessity to bridge the gap created by falling loan disbursements. The one number to watch is the USD/BDT median; if it consistently breaches Tk 124.00 despite central bank resistance, the managed float is failing.

**So what:** Bangladesh is losing dollars through trade and debt faster than it can attract them through loans, making a taka devaluation more likely.

<sub>links #3, #4, #12 · strong</sub>

Global natural gas price surge → Domestic grid failure (gas plunge) → 25-30% RMG capacity loss.

**So what:** High energy costs are not just a fiscal problem; they are physically stopping factories from producing goods, which will eventually hurt export earnings.

<sub>links #1, #9 · plausible</sub>

**So what:** The government is forced to seek new emergency loans just to pay back old ones and keep the lights on.
