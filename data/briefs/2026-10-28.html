Today's lesson

Concept

Cost-Push Inflation

Usually, prices go up because people have too much money and want to buy things (demand-pull). Cost-push inflation is different. It happens when the *cost* of making things goes up, forcing companies to raise prices just to survive.

Today’s 13% jump in gas prices is a perfect example. Because gas is used for electricity and factory machines, almost every business in Bangladesh now faces higher costs. Unilever already reported raising prices because of energy.

When the Taka falls, it adds a second layer: the raw materials we buy from abroad also cost more. This creates a cycle where prices rise even if people aren't spending more.

Test yourselfIf the Taka gets stronger but oil prices double, would that cause cost-push or demand-pull inflation? (Answer: Cost-push, because the input cost of energy has risen.)

Jargon decoder

These terms appear in today's brief. Hover any dotted word above to see its meaning without scrolling back.

Force majeure

A legal clause that frees a company from a contract because of an event they can't control, like a war or a natural disaster.

Qatar used this to explain why it can't deliver gas to Bangladesh through 2026.

It is the 'act of God' clause that lets suppliers walk away when things get tough.

Non-performing loan (NPL)

A loan where the borrower has stopped making payments for a long time, usually 90 days.

Wahiduddin Mahmud argued that the banking crisis is driven by people intentionally 'plundering' these funds.

An NPL is a 'dead' asset that stops a bank from lending to anyone else.

Capital shortfall

When a bank doesn't have enough of its own money to protect its depositors if its loans go bad.

Bangladesh Bank is giving banks until 2027 to fix these shortfalls.

It is the difference between a bank being a safe vault and a house of cards.

Export factoring

When a business sells its export invoices to a bank at a discount to get cash immediately.

Mercantile Bank signed a deal with Tradewind to offer this service to Bangladeshi exporters.

It is like getting a payday loan for your business so you don't have to wait 90 days for a buyer to pay.
