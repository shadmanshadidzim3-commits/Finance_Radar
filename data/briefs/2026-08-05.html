Today's lesson

Concept

Negative Real Interest Rates (Financial Repression)

When the rate of inflation exceeds the interest rate paid on bank deposits, savers experience negative real interest rates. In Bangladesh today, inflation runs above 9%, while commercial banks are reducing deposit interest rates below 7% following the central bank's policy rate cut. Financially, this functions as a hidden tax on cash savings.

Purchasing power moves away from household savers toward borrowers and banks. To avoid losing real wealth, rational savers reduce bank deposits and buy physical assets like land, gold, or foreign currency, or send money through informal hundi channels.

Net interest margin

The gap between what a bank earns lending money and what it pays savers. It is where nearly all bank profit comes from.

Commercial banks are cutting deposit rates faster than loan rates to protect their net interest margins after Bangladesh Bank reduced the policy rate.

Think of it as a store's markup: the wider the gap between buying money and selling money, the more profit the bank keeps.

Real interest rate

The interest rate you receive on savings after subtracting the inflation rate.

Banks pushed deposit returns further below inflation today, making real interest rates even more negative for Bangladeshi savers.

If your interest rate is lower than inflation, your bank account is losing purchasing power every month.

Private sector credit growth

The speed at which businesses are taking out new bank loans to expand operations.

This metric hit a 33-year low today, proving that Bangladeshi companies are holding back on building new factories or buying equipment.

It is the economy's confidence score: high borrowing means expansion, low borrowing means corporate retreat.

Fiscal deficit

The shortfall when government spending exceeds tax revenues over a year.

The NBR missing its collection target by Tk 88,000 crore directly widens Bangladesh's fiscal deficit.

When the government spends $100 but only collects $80 in taxes, the $20 gap is the fiscal deficit that must be borrowed.

Capacity utilization rate

The percentage of a factory's full production potential that is actually being used.

Plastic manufacturers reported running at just 30% capacity due to national gas supply shortages.

A low capacity utilization rate means expensive machines sit idle while fixed overhead costs eat up business profit.
