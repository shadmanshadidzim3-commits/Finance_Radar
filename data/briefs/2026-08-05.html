Today's lesson

Concept

Real negative interest rates

When you deposit money in a bank, the bank pays you an interest rate called the nominal rate. However, prices of goods continuously rise due to inflation. To calculate whether your saved money is gaining or losing actual buying power, you must subtract the inflation rate from the bank's nominal deposit rate.

The result is the real interest rate. Today, Bangladeshi banks pay deposit rates lower than the official inflation rate. When the real interest rate becomes negative, leaving money in a bank account means your savings buy less every year.

This incentivizes savers to pull cash out of banks to buy real estate, gold, or foreign currency, leaving banks with fewer deposits to lend to productive businesses.
