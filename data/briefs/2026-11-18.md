## The day in one read

Today was defined by the formal acknowledgment of structural failures in both the banking sector and the national accounts. The single dominant force is the central bank's decision to put Padma Bank under resolution, a move that signals the end of 'extend and pretend' policies for insolvent lenders. This happens just as a government audit admitted that past GDP growth was inflated, forcing a reset of the country's economic baseline.

What changed versus recent days is the shift from managing labor unrest to managing systemic solvency. While earlier notes tracked factory protests as an operational risk, the combination of the GDP audit and the IMF's new focus on interest bills suggests a tightening fiscal trap. The government has less money to spend, less growth than it thought, and more expensive debt to pay.

What to watch next is the RMG wage board meeting on November 23. The gap between the CPD's proposed Tk 18,578 and the owners' current stance is the most critical number for the economy. If a deal isn't reached, the closure of 132 factories could become a permanent loss of export capacity. Watch the foreign exchange reserve level; if it continues to drop despite the $200 million injection, a sharper Taka devaluation is inevitable.
