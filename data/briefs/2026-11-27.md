## The day in one read

Today was defined by the formal recognition of a competitiveness crisis in Bangladesh's garment sector. The $254 million shift in orders to India is not just a number; it is a signal that global brands are losing patience with domestic labor unrest and rising costs. While the Tk 17,000 wage floor is now set, the industry is simultaneously facing an 18% US tariff threat and a 6.6% spike in global gas prices. This is a classic cost-push squeeze where input prices rise while market access narrows.

What changed today is the quantification of the banking sector's rot alongside these trade pressures. Defaulted loans have officially hit Tk 6.27 lakh crore, or 33.8% of all lending. This confirms that the financial system is essentially operating with one-third of its capital frozen in bad debt. The central bank's move to cap cash dollar rates is a tactical response to this underlying scarcity, attempting to keep the retail market from reflecting the true depth of the dollar shortage.

Watch the USD/BDT spread in the coming days. While the median rate is Tk 124.88, the 0.61 taka spread between providers indicates that the market is struggling to find a stable price. If the spread widens further, it will signal that the central bank's caps are being ignored by the informal market. The one number to track is the $3.485 natural gas price; if it stays above $3.40, expect power tariff hikes by January.
