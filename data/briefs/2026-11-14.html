Today's lesson

Concept

The Denominator Effect in Sovereign Solvency

A sovereign country's creditworthiness is rarely judged by debt levels in isolation. Lenders evaluate ratios, primarily public debt divided by gross domestic product (GDP). Debt is the numerator; GDP is the denominator.

Today's forensic audit revealed that Bangladesh overstated annual GDP growth by up to 1.30 percentage points over a decade. Even if nominal debt does not rise by a single taka, recalculating national output downward shrinks the denominator. A smaller denominator automatically increases the debt-to-GDP ratio.

This makes the country appear more leveraged to credit rating agencies and international bondholders, pushing up future sovereign borrowing costs.
