Today's lesson

Concept

The Trade Deficit Transmission Channel

A trade deficit occurs when a nation imports more merchandise than it exports. When Bangladesh buys crude oil, liquefied natural gas, and machinery, it must pay international suppliers in US dollars. If apparel export proceeds do not match this import total, the banking system runs a net dollar deficit.

Commercial banks then turn to Bangladesh Bank to purchase dollars. The central bank must either sell foreign exchange reserves to defend the taka, or let the exchange rate depreciate. Depreciation makes future imports even more expensive, feeding imported inflation.

Thus, a widening trade deficit acts as an automatic drain on foreign exchange reserves and a trigger for currency depreciation.

Trade deficit

The amount by which a country's spending on imports exceeds its earnings from exports over a given period.

The Dhaka Tribune reported that Bangladesh's trade deficit widened by 34%, hitting a three-year peak.

Think of it as buying more from your local shops than you earn at your job — you must cover the difference with savings or debt.

Foreign exchange reserves

The stockpile of foreign currencies held by a central bank to back liabilities and guarantee payment for imports.

A 34% wider trade deficit directly drains Bangladesh Bank's reserve buffer to settle incoming letters of credit.

It is the nation's emergency cash buffer against international payment defaults.

Green bond

A fixed-interest debt instrument issued specifically to raise money for environmentally positive or climate-resilient projects.

BRAC Bank announced a Tk 1,000 crore green bond yielding 7.5% to finance clean industrial assets.

A regular IOU, but the borrower legally promises to spend the funds only on eco-friendly assets.

Annual Development Programme (ADP)

The government's yearly budget allocation designated specifically for physical infrastructure projects, roads, and bridges.

ADP execution dropped to a record low of 3.89% in the first quarter of the current fiscal year.

It is the state's capital expenditure pipeline — when it stalls, domestic industrial demand dries up.
