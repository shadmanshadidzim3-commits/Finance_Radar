Today's lesson

Concept

The twin deficit problem

A twin deficit occurs when an economy runs both a fiscal deficit (the government spends more than its revenue) and a current account deficit (the country imports more goods and services than it exports). When energy prices jump, the government must pay massive fuel subsidies, widening the fiscal budget shortfall. At the exact same time, paying foreign suppliers for that expensive fuel requires hard currency, widening the trade and current account deficits.

To finance the fiscal gap, the state borrows heavily from commercial banks, driving up domestic interest rates. To finance the external gap, the central bank must run down foreign exchange reserves. When both deficits expand together, currency depreciation and interest rate spikes reinforce each other.

Jargon decoder

Fiscal deficit

The shortfall when a government spends more money in a year than it collects in taxes and revenues. It must borrow to cover the difference.

A projected Tk 40,000 crore LNG subsidy bill directly threatens to expand Bangladesh's fiscal deficit.

Think of it as the national overdraft: every taka spent beyond tax collection must be borrowed from banks or abroad.

Current account deficit

A measure of whether a country is spending more on foreign trade and transfers than it is earning from exports and remittances.

A 38% wider trade deficit in July pushed the current account deeper into negative territory.

It is a nation's cash flow statement against the rest of the world; a deficit means cash is leaving the country.

Core inflation

The rate of price increases excluding food and energy costs, showing the underlying persistent trend in living costs.

Domestic core inflation posted its largest gain in four months, signalling sticky price pressures beyond temporary food shocks.

Strip out food and fuel volatility to see if overall prices are genuinely stabilizing or quietly spiraling.

Non-performing loan (NPL)

A bank loan where the borrower has stopped paying interest or principal repayments for at least 90 days.

Bangladesh Bank enlisted 131 independent asset valuation firms specifically to stop borrowers from inflating collateral to secure bad loans.

A dead asset on the bank balance sheet that stops producing income and eats into capital reserves.

Effective protection rate

The net tariff advantage given to a local manufacturer, measured by taxing finished imported goods while letting raw materials in cheaper.

The World Bank urged Bangladesh to gradually dismantle high effective protection rates to boost industrial competitiveness.

It is a regulatory tariff wall that shields local factories from foreign competition but raises consumer prices.
