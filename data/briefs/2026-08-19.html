Today's lesson

Concept

Counterparty Risk in Institutional Deposits

When an entity deposits cash in a commercial bank, the accounting entry records an asset (cash equivalent) on the depositor's balance sheet and a liability (deposit) on the bank's balance sheet. The depositor assumes the bank is liquid and solvent enough to return the cash upon request. Counterparty risk is the probability that the bank cannot fulfill this obligation due to insolvency, illiquidity, or bad loans.

In corporate finance, placing large deposits in weak institutions carrying high non-performing loans creates severe asset lockups, as seen with DESCO's Tk 250 crore trapped in troubled lenders. Depositors must evaluate bank creditworthiness, not just interest rates.

Credit risk

The risk that a borrower will fail to pay back a loan on time. It is the primary reason banks suffer financial losses.

Thirty-eight commercial banks requested Tk 28,000 crore in central bank support because corporate borrowers are struggling to service debt.

Ask: 'If the borrower's factory slows down, who absorbs the unpaid bill?'

Regasification

The industrial process of warming liquefied natural gas back into gas so it can flow through domestic pipelines.

The Excelerate offshore terminal halted regasification, cutting fuel supplies to factories and power plants.

You cannot burn liquid gas in a domestic power plant; it must be regasified first.

Counterparty risk

The likelihood that the other party in a financial agreement will default on its contractual obligations.

DESCO cannot access Tk 250 crore of its deposits held across six troubled commercial banks.

A deposit is an asset to you, but a liability to the bank; if the bank fails, your asset is trapped.

Gross margin

The share of sales revenue left over after paying the direct costs of manufacturing a product.

Surging global cotton and fuel prices increase raw material expenses, reducing the gross profit margin of garment exporters.

Gross margin is revenue minus direct factory costs — it tells you if the core product is profitable.

Average revenue per user

A standard performance metric that calculates how much revenue a company earns from each active customer each month.

To grow revenue without adding new subscribers, a company must raise its average revenue per user.
