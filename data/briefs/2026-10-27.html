Today's lesson

Concept

Negative Net Inflow

In development finance, a country usually expects to receive more in new loans than it pays back in old ones. This 'positive net inflow' helps the economy grow. Today's data shows Bangladesh has flipped into a 'negative net inflow' state, paying $2.04 for every $1 received.

This happens when old debts (principal and interest) come due at the same time that new lenders become cautious or project disbursements slow down. For a country like Bangladesh, which needs dollars to buy fuel and food, exporting more cash to creditors than it receives is a major drag on the economy and a primary cause of currency weakness.

Test yourselfIf a country receives $500 million in new loans but pays $1 billion in debt servicing, is its net inflow positive or negative? (Answer: Negative, by $500 million).

Debt servicing

The cash required to pay back the principal and interest on outstanding loans.

Bangladesh is currently paying out $2.04 for every $1 it receives in new loans.

It is like paying 2,000 taka on your credit card but only being allowed to borrow 1,000 taka more.

Trade deficit

When a country buys more from the world than it sells to the world.

The deficit hit a three-year high of $27.29 billion, a 34% increase.

A trade deficit is a bill that must eventually be paid in foreign currency.

Capacity loss

The portion of a factory's potential output that it cannot produce.

RMG factories are losing 25-30% of their production because the power is out.

If you have 10 machines but can only run 7 because of blackouts, your capacity loss is 30%.

Budget support

A loan given to a government that can be spent on anything in the national budget.

The government is asking South Korea for $1 billion to help manage energy costs.

Unlike a project loan for a specific bridge, budget support is like an emergency cash injection for the treasury.
