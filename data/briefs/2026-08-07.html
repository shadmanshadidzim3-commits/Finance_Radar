Today's lesson

Concept

Negative Real Interest Rates

Interest rates represent the price of money. When you put money in a bank deposit, you earn a nominal interest rate. However, inflation continuously reduces the purchasing power of that money.

The real interest rate equals the nominal interest rate minus the inflation rate. If a bank pays you 7% interest on a fixed deposit, but headline inflation is running at 9%, your real interest rate is -2%. Your account balance grows on paper, but the cash buys 2% fewer goods at the end of the year.

Today, Bangladeshi commercial banks slashed deposit yields below inflation following Bangladesh Bank's policy cut. When real returns turn negative, rational depositors stop saving in banks. They move capital into physical assets, foreign currencies, gold, or speculative equity trades, draining stable liquidity from the commercial banking system.
