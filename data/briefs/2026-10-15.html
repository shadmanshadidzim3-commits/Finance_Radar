Today's lesson

Concept

Contingent Liabilities and Sovereign Guarantees

A sovereign guarantee is a legally binding commitment by the state or central bank to assume a debt if the primary borrower defaults. When Bangladesh Bank guarantees a Tk 7,000 crore interbank loan between Sonali Bank and National Bank, that money does not appear as an immediate expenditure in the national budget. Instead, it enters public accounts as a contingent liability — an obligation that crystallises only upon default.

If the borrowing bank recovers and repays the funds, the state spends nothing. But if the borrower fails, the central bank must absorb the losses, either by printing currency or drawing on public funds. Sovereign guarantees prevent immediate depositor runs, but they quietly transfer private commercial insolvency risk onto taxpayers.
