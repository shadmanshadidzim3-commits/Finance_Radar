**2. The government repurchased Tk 1,716 crore of two-year treasury bonds in Bangladesh's first-ever sovereign buyback auction.**

A bond buyback occurs when a sovereign issuer repurchases its outstanding debt before maturity, injecting high-powered liquidity back into primary dealer commercial banks. This active liability management smooths out debt maturity clusters and prevents sharp refinancing spikes. For participating banks, liquidating sovereign paper frees up cash reserves to meet commercial lending or deposit withdrawal obligations.

**4. Bangladesh Bank expanded Non-Resident Taka Account privileges, allowing local payment clearing and bank lending.**

A Non-Resident Taka Account (NRCTA) holds domestic currency converted from foreign remittances sent by overseas citizens. Allowing banks to deploy these balances into loan portfolios expands deposit bases and improves credit creation without drawing on interbank liquidity. For account holders, direct domestic clearing provides convertibility incentives that channel funds away from the informal hundi network.
