## The day in one read

Today was characterized by a significant policy shift toward economic liberalization as Bangladesh Bank moved to ease capital controls. The single dominant force was the central bank's decision to allow dividend repatriation and ease preference share rules, a clear attempt to lure back foreign currency that has fled the country during the recent dollar crisis.

What changed versus recent days is the government's move to resolve industrial unrest through a formal wage board agreement. While previous notes focused on the physical shutdown of factories, today's news shifts the focus to the long-term cost of that peace: higher operating expenses for exporters. This comes just as the IMF intensifies pressure for a VAT overhaul, signaling that the era of cheap energy and low taxes is ending.

What to watch next is whether the unfreezing of BO accounts actually translates into higher DSE turnover or if investors remain spooked by the widening spread in the USD/BDT rate. The one number that will tell the story is the provider spread in the Taka market; if it stays above 0.50 taka, it means the interbank market is still not functioning efficiently.
