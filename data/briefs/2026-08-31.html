Today's lesson

Concept

Fiscal Crowding Out

When a government runs a large budget deficit — spending more than it collects in taxes — it must borrow money to bridge the gap. In Bangladesh, where foreign loans are constrained, the state borrows directly from domestic commercial banks by selling Treasury bills and bonds. Because banks have a finite pool of customer deposits, every taka lent to the government is a taka unavailable to lend to private factories, real estate, or small businesses.

This is called 'crowding out.' Because lending to the sovereign is risk-free for banks, commercial lenders prefer holding government paper over making riskier commercial loans. As a result, private companies face higher interest rates and credit shortages, curbing private sector investment and employment.

Current account deficit

The gap when a country spends more foreign currency on imports and overseas services than it earns from exports and remittances.

Spiking oil prices and rising cotton costs widen Bangladesh's import bill, deepening balance of payments stress.

Think of it as a national overdraft: spending more abroad than you bring in from outside.

Crowding-out effect

When heavy government borrowing from commercial banks uses up available credit, leaving little money for private businesses to borrow.

The government borrowed Tk 165,538 crore from domestic banks, Tk 47,500 crore above plan, constraining private loan growth.

When the government takes the biggest seat at the bank counter, private borrowers get pushed out the door.

Turnover tax

A mandatory tax charged directly on a company's total gross sales, regardless of whether the business generated a profit or suffered a loss.

The NBR exempted firms with turnover under Tk 2 crore from this levy to support small business cash flows.

A tax on top-line revenue that bites even when you are losing money.

Direct listing

A process where an established company lists its existing shares on the stock exchange without issuing new shares or hiring underwriters.

Opening the front door to the stock market without running an expensive IPO sales roadshow.
