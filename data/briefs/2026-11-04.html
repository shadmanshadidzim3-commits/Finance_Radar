Today's lesson

Concept

The Impossible Trinity

Today's news illustrates the 'Impossible Trinity' (or Trilemma), which states that a country cannot have all three at once: a fixed exchange rate, free capital movement, and an independent monetary policy. Bangladesh currently tries to manage the Taka (fixed-ish exchange rate) and set its own interest rates (independent monetary policy). To do this, it has historically restricted the flow of money (capital controls).

However, today's move to ease dividend repatriation and preference share rules shows the central bank is trying to allow more capital movement. The trade-off? It will likely have to let the Taka float more freely or lose control over domestic interest rates to keep the system balanced.

Test yourselfIf Bangladesh Bank allows completely free movement of dollars in and out of the country, what must it give up to maintain control over its own interest rates? (Answer: It must give up the fixed exchange rate and let the Taka find its own market value.)

Jargon decoder

These terms appear in today's brief. Hover any dotted word above to see its meaning without scrolling back.

Capital account

The part of a country's balance of payments that records all transactions made between entities in one country and the rest of the world.

Bangladesh Bank is easing capital account restrictions to allow foreign investors to take their profits home more easily.

Think of it as the 'exit door' for money; if the door is locked, new money won't come in through the entrance.

Preference shares

A type of stock that pays a fixed dividend and has priority over common stock if a company goes bankrupt.

New rules allow these to be counted as equity capital, helping banks look healthier on paper.

It's like a hybrid between a loan and a stock—you get a fixed payout like a loan, but you own a piece of the company like a stock.

Market breadth

A technique used in technical analysis that looks at the number of stocks participating in a market move.

BSEC is unfreezing accounts to increase the number of active participants in the market.

A rally with high breadth is a healthy crowd; a rally with low breadth is just a few big players moving the needle.

Fiscal consolidation

Government policies aimed at reducing the budget deficit and the accumulation of debt.

The IMF is pushing the government to raise taxes and cut subsidies to achieve this.

It is the national version of 'tightening your belt' to pay off a credit card.
