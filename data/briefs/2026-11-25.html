Today's lesson

Concept

The Wage-Price Spiral

The wage-price spiral is a macroeconomic theory explaining a cause-and-effect relationship between rising wages and rising prices (inflation). When workers receive a significant wage hike—like the RMG sector's move to Tk 17,000—businesses face higher production costs. To maintain their profit margins, they raise the prices of their goods.

This leads to higher overall inflation in the economy. Workers then find that their new, higher wages don't buy as much as they expected, leading them to demand even higher wages. In Bangladesh's case, because we export garments, we can't easily raise prices for global buyers, so the 'spiral' instead leads to factory closures or reduced hiring.

Margin compression

When the costs of making a product rise faster than the price you can sell it for, leaving you with less profit.

It's like having a bigger appetite but a smaller plate.

Crowding out

When the government borrows so much money from banks that there isn't enough left for private businesses to borrow.

Govt borrowing from banks surged 72% because tax revenue fell short.

If the government takes the last seat on the bus, the businessman has to walk.

Debt securitisation

Turning a bundle of loans into a bond that can be bought and sold like a stock.

GPH Ispat is looking to turn Tk 12,000 crore of bank debt into bonds to manage its liabilities.

It's turning a private IOU into a public ticket.

Import duty

A tax the government collects on goods coming into the country from abroad.

The NBR cut duties on fruits to keep prices lower during Ramadan.

It's the 'entry fee' for foreign goods.
