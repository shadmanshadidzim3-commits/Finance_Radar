Today's lesson

Concept

Escrow account segregation in corporate governance

When a public corporation declares a dividend, it incurs a binding liability to shareholders. In liquidity-constrained corporate sectors, cash-poor companies often declare dividends during their annual general meetings to support their share prices, but quietly divert that cash to pay short-term working capital expenses or bank interest. By the time distribution day arrives, the cash is gone, leading to delayed payouts and investor disputes.

By mandating an escrow bank account — a legally partitioned account dedicated exclusively to dividend distribution — regulators ensure funds are ring-fenced the moment they are declared. This practice forces management to treat shareholder distributions as an unalterable cash outflow rather than an internal credit line.
