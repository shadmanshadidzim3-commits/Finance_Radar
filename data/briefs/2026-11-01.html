Today's lesson

Concept

Unit labor cost and export competitiveness

A country's export competitiveness depends not just on absolute wage rates, but on unit labor cost. Unit labor cost measures the total nominal wage bill divided by total real output. If a Bangladeshi garment worker earns Tk 12,500 monthly and sews 500 shirts, the unit labor cost is Tk 25 per shirt.

If wages double to Tk 25,000 without capital upgrades or higher output, the unit labor cost doubles to Tk 50 per shirt. Buyers will then shift purchase orders to lower-cost competitors like Vietnam or Cambodia unless factory automation, logistics efficiency, or scale offsets the higher wage expense.

A bank loan where the borrower has stopped making scheduled interest or principal payments for 90 days or more.

Rupali Bank revealed that half of its Tk 19,805 crore defaulted loan book is concentrated in just 20 borrowers.

An NPL is an asset that stopped generating cash flow and now consumes bank capital.

Current account deficit

The shortfall when a country spends more on foreign goods, services, and overseas transfers than it receives from exports and remittances.

It is the nation's monthly trade checkbook: negative means you must borrow dollars to bridge the gap.

Net interest margin (NIM)

The percentage difference between what a bank earns on loans and securities versus what it pays depositors.
