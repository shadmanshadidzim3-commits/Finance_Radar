Today's lesson

Concept

Trade Credit as a Macro Shock Absorber

When an economy suffers from a shortage of foreign currency, central banks often face a binary choice: either sell scarce official reserves to fund essential imports or allow domestic shortages and price spikes. Deferred payment letters of credit provide a third path known as trade credit. By allowing domestic importers to take delivery of commodities today and defer foreign exchange settlement by 90 to 180 days, the central bank transfers immediate spot foreign exchange demand into the future.

While this prevents immediate exchange rate spikes and curbs seasonal inflation, it does not erase the liability. It creates an accumulated maturity wall. If export earnings and remittances do not accelerate before those deferred bills mature, the future dollar drain can be even more violent.

Jargon decoder

These terms appear in today's brief. Hover any dotted word above to see its meaning without scrolling back.

Letter of credit (LC)

A formal bank guarantee promising that an overseas seller will be paid on time once goods are shipped.

Commercial banks settled import LCs at rates approaching Tk 125 today as fuel import bills arrived.

Think of an LC as a financial escrow agent: the bank stands in the middle so strangers across borders can trust each other.

Trade credit

An arrangement where a buyer takes delivery of goods immediately but pays the supplier weeks or months later.

Bangladesh Bank granted 90-day deferred payment terms for Ramadan food imports, effectively forcing suppliers to provide trade credit.

Buy now, pay later for international commerce.

Real wages

Income measured by the actual basket of goods and services it can buy, after stripping out the effect of inflation.

Garment workers are experiencing severe real wage cuts because food prices have risen faster than nominal wages.

Nominal wage is what is written on your pay slip; real wage is what is inside your grocery bag.

Operating leverage

How much a company's operating profit increases when its sales grow, determined by the proportion of fixed costs it carries.

Square Pharmaceuticals posted higher profits because strong sales growth outpaced its fixed operational overhead.

High operating leverage is a double-edged sword: profits surge when sales rise, but collapse when sales drop.

Know Your Customer (KYC)

Mandatory background and identity checks that financial institutions must conduct before opening an account.

The central bank investigated bKash after identifying 7,000 accounts that violated identity verification standards.

The banking equivalent of checking an ID at the border to prevent illegal financial flows.
