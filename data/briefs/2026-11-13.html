Today's lesson

Concept

The Impossible Trinity

The Impossible Trinity (or Trilemma) states that a country cannot have all three of these at the same time: a fixed exchange rate, free capital movement, and an independent monetary policy. Bangladesh is currently trapped in this. It wants to keep the Taka stable (fixed rate) to prevent inflation, but it also needs to move money for trade (capital flow).

Because the US is raising interest rates, Bangladesh is forced to either let the Taka fall or raise its own interest rates to match, which hurts local businesses. You can't fight the global market and keep your own rates low without running out of dollars.

Jargon decoder

These terms appear in today's brief. Hover any dotted word above to see its meaning without scrolling back.

Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR)

A measure of a bank's available capital expressed as a percentage of its risk-weighted credit exposure.

Islami Bank's request for Tk 6,000 crore is a direct attempt to fix its failing CAR.

It is the 'safety cushion' that prevents a bank from going bust when loans go bad.

Fiscal Deficit

The shortfall in a government's income compared with its spending.

The massive losses at BPC and power subsidies are the primary drivers of the national deficit right now.

When this number gets too high, the government has to print money or borrow, both of which cause inflation.

Hundi

An informal and illegal money transfer system that bypasses the official banking channels.

The Tk 129 open market dollar rate makes hundi more attractive than sending money through banks.

Hundi is a 'dollar drain' that keeps foreign currency out of the national reserves.

Risk-Free Rate

The theoretical rate of return of an investment with zero risk, usually represented by US government bonds.

The US 10-year yield hitting 5.1% means the global risk-free rate is rising, making Bangladesh look riskier by comparison.

When the risk-free rate goes up, the price of everything else—stocks, houses, and emerging market currencies—usually goes down.
