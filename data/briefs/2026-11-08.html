Today's lesson

Concept

Cash margin requirements on Letters of Credit

When an importer purchases goods internationally, its bank issues a Letter of Credit (LC) guaranteeing payment to the overseas exporter. To manage default and currency risk, the central bank specifies a 'cash margin' — the percentage of the order's value the buyer must deposit upfront in cash. If the margin is 100%, the importer must lock up the entire value before the deal starts.

When Bangladesh Bank lowers the margin to near zero for Ramadan essentials, it removes this cash drag. Importers can bring in rice or oil using less upfront cash, boosting domestic supply. However, this policy increases immediate demand for foreign currency in the interbank market.

Trade deficit

The amount by which a country's total spending on foreign imports exceeds its earnings from exported goods.

Bangladesh's trade deficit expanded by 31% in the first quarter due to a doubled fuel import bill.

A trade deficit means more money left the country for goods than came in.

Cash margin

The upfront cash a business must deposit with its bank before the bank agrees to back an import purchase.

Bangladesh Bank ordered lenders to keep cash margins at minimum levels for ten Ramadan food commodities.

It is the earnest money an importer puts on the counter before goods ship.

Working capital facility

A revolving bank loan used to pay for day-to-day operating expenses like inventory, raw materials, and wages.

Bangladesh Bank cleared a Tk 3,000 crore working capital line for defaulted firms within City Group.

It funds everyday operational costs, not new factories or long-term machinery.

Per-capita external debt

A country's total foreign debt obligations divided evenly across its entire population.

World Bank data showed external debt per citizen reached $646 across 175.7 million Bangladeshis.

It gauges the country's foreign debt burden per head, even though individuals do not repay it directly.
