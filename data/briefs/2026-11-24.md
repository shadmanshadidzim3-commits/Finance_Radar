**4. Six state-owned commercial banks accumulated unlent deposits as lending ground to a halt, according to a finance ministry report.**

According to The Daily Star, state-run lenders are parking fresh funds in risk-free government securities rather than creating private credit. This dynamic compresses net interest margin — the spread between interest earned on loans and interest paid to savers. Banks preserve liquidity buffers to guard against default shocks, which in turn chokes business investment across the real economy.

**5. Bangladesh Bank announced plans to issue Tk 32,000 crore in Sukuk in FY27 to ease Islamic banking liquidity shortages.**

According to The Business Standard, this planned issuance provides Islamic banks with an eligible statutory liquidity reserve asset — government-approved paper banks must hold to meet regulatory safety rules. Islamic lenders in Bangladesh have suffered acute liquidity shortfalls and lacked conventional central bank repurchase facilities. Structured Sukuk issuances absorb excess cash and finance public infrastructure without violating Islamic financing prohibitions.
