Today's lesson

Concept

The Bank Resolution Framework

When a standard corporation fails, court judges manage bankruptcy over years. When a financial institution fails, regulators cannot wait because panic spreads instantly through the financial system. A bank resolution framework grants central banks special powers to intervene immediately without court delays.

The central bank can suspend share trading, write off shareholder capital, transfer healthy deposits to solvent banks, and liquidate remaining assets. The core accounting principle during resolution is the statutory creditor hierarchy: secured depositors get paid first, uncollateralized bondholders second, and equity shareholders last. Today's liquidation of four NBFIs is Bangladesh Bank using this exact regulatory power.

Current account deficit

The shortfall when a country spends more on imported goods and foreign payments than it earns from exports and remittances.

It is the economy's checkbook balance with the rest of the world.

Moratorium

An official delay or temporary pause granted on paying debts or bills.

Chittagong industrialists requested a 90-day moratorium on energy bills and bank debt servicing.

A moratorium pauses the payment deadline, but it does not erase the debt.

Monopsony

A market where there is only one buyer for a product or service.

Private fuel import proposals seek to end BPC's monopsony over oil procurement.

A monopoly controls what you buy; a monopsony controls what you sell.

Pass-through

Transferring higher production or import costs directly onto the prices consumers pay.

BERC increased jet fuel prices by 23% to pass high international procurement costs to airlines.

Pass-through determines how much of an import price shock lands on your receipt.
