Today's lesson

Concept

Cost-Push Inflation

Usually, prices go up because people have too much money and want to buy everything (demand-pull). Cost-push inflation is the opposite: it happens when the *cost* of making things goes up, forcing companies to raise prices or go out of business. Today, Bangladesh is seeing this in real-time.

Natural gas (energy) is 6.6% more expensive, and garment wages are up to Tk 17,000. Because factories have to pay more for power and workers, they must charge more for shirts. If they can't, their profit margins disappear.

If they do, buyers like Zara might go to India instead. It's inflation driven by 'pain' at the factory level, not 'wealth' at the consumer level.

Test yourselfIf global oil prices fall but domestic wages rise, is the pressure on a factory's margin increasing or decreasing? Answer: It depends on which cost is larger, but the wage hike is a 'cost-push' factor that increases pressure.

Jargon decoder

These terms appear in today's brief. Hover any dotted word above to see its meaning without scrolling back.

Non-performing loan (NPL)

A loan where the borrower has stopped making payments for at least 90 days.

NPLs reached a record Tk 6.27 lakh crore, showing that 33.8% of the banking system's money is stuck.

Think of NPLs as 'zombie money'—it's on the books, but it isn't moving or helping the economy.

BC Selling Rate

The exchange rate banks use when a company pays for imported goods.

Bangladesh Bank capped the price of physical cash dollars at this rate to stop price gouging.

It is the 'wholesale' price of dollars; the central bank now wants the 'retail' price to match it.

Operating Cash Flow

The actual cash a company brings in from its day-to-day business, excluding loans or investments.

BSEC will now use this to decide if a company is healthy enough to join the stock market.

Profit is what you tell the taxman; cash flow is what you use to pay your bills.

Debt Servicing

The cash required to pay back the interest and principal on a loan for a specific period.

The World Bank warned that too much of Bangladesh's tax revenue is being eaten up by these payments.

It's like a household spending half its salary just on credit card interest.
