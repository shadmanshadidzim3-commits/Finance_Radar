Today's lesson

Concept

Forensic Audit of Macro Data

In finance, we usually trust official government data as the 'ground truth.' However, a forensic audit uses accounting techniques to look behind the headlines. Today's news that GDP was overstated by 1.3% annually since 2015 is a classic example.

By checking industrial production reports against tax filings and export data, auditors found the numbers didn't add up. For an investor, this is a 'denominator problem.' If GDP is smaller than we thought, then our debt-to-GDP ratio (how much we owe vs. what we earn) is higher.

This makes the country look riskier to lenders, which can lead to higher interest rates on the sovereign bonds the government plans to issue in January.

Test yourselfIf a country's debt is $100 and its GDP is reported as $200, but an audit shows GDP is actually $180, does the debt-to-GDP ratio get better or worse? (Answer: Worse, it rises from 50% to 55.5%).

Jargon decoder

Non-performing loan (NPL)

A loan where the borrower has not made scheduled payments for a specific period, usually 90 days.

15 banks were identified as holding 85% of the country's Tk 600,000 crore in NPLs.

NPLs are 'zombie money'—it's on the books as an asset, but it isn't actually coming back to the bank.

GDP base year

The specific year used as a benchmark to calculate economic growth while removing the effects of inflation.

The government is shifting to a FY26 base year to improve data accuracy after the audit revealed past inflation of growth figures.

Changing the base year is like recalibrating a scale to make sure it's showing your true weight, not just a relative change.

Sovereign bond

A certificate of debt issued by a national government to raise money in foreign currencies from international investors.

Bangladesh is planning its first-ever international sovereign bond issue in January to meet debt obligations.

It is the government's 'credit card' on the global stage; the interest rate we pay depends on how much the world trusts our economy.

Margin compression

When a company's costs rise faster than its sales prices, leaving it with less profit per item sold.

Rising cotton prices and wage demands are threatening to squeeze the profits of RMG exporters.

It's the feeling of being caught between a floor that's rising (costs) and a ceiling that's stuck (sale price).
