Today's lesson

Concept

The Credit Crunch

A credit crunch happens when commercial banks suddenly reduce their lending activity, not because businesses do not want loans, but because banks are terrified of default risks. In Bangladesh today, six state banks are collecting large volumes of customer deposits but choosing to hold excess cash or purchase risk-free treasury bills instead of lending to private firms. When non-performing loans surge, regulators force banks to set aside capital provisions against potential losses.

To protect their capital adequacy, banks ration credit to private companies. Consequently, sound businesses cannot borrow to fund working capital or buy machinery, causing real investment and industrial output to stall.
