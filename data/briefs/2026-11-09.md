## The day in one read

Today was defined by the stark reality of Bangladesh's external account pressure. The reported drop in foreign exchange reserves to below $30 billion following the $1.64 billion ACU payment is the single most important force. This drain, coupled with a $7.47 billion trade deficit in the first quarter, confirms that the country is spending far beyond its dollar earnings.

What changed versus recent days is the emergence of a clear 'corporate decoupling.' While the macro indicators are grim, companies like Envoy Textiles are reporting 40% profit growth by switching to cheaper foreign financing. This suggests that the 'winners' in this market will be firms that can access multilateral credit (like ADB or WB funds) to bypass the high interest rates and dollar scarcity affecting the rest of the economy.

What to watch next is the RMG wage board's response to the Tk 32,000 minimum wage proposal. If labor costs rise sharply while energy shortages continue to cut production by 30%, the export engine will face a severe margin squeeze. The one number that will tell us if the situation is stabilizing is the corroborated reserve level; if it continues to slide toward $25 billion, expect more aggressive capital controls.
