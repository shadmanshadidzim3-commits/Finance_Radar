Today's lesson

Concept

Cost-Push Inflation

Inflation is usually caused by one of two things: people having too much money to spend (demand-pull) or the cost of making things going up (cost-push). Today, Bangladesh is facing classic cost-push inflation. When Brent crude hits $116, every truck that moves food and every factory that makes clothes becomes more expensive to run.

These businesses pass those costs on to you. Unlike demand-pull inflation, which you can fix by raising interest rates to stop people from spending, cost-push inflation is harder to fight because people still need to buy essentials like food and fuel regardless of the price.

Test yourselfIf the price of bread rises because the cost of diesel for delivery trucks went up, is that demand-pull or cost-push inflation? (Answer: Cost-push).

Cost-push inflation

When prices go up because it costs companies more to make things, usually due to higher fuel or raw material costs.

It's inflation caused by the supply side (costs) rather than the demand side (spending).

Direct Listing

A way for a company to put its shares on the stock market without a traditional public sale or hiring expensive investment banks.

BSEC is making the rules for this process stricter to protect investors from bad companies.

Sukuk

An Islamic financial certificate, similar to a bond, that complies with Shariah law by representing ownership in an asset.

The government is looking at Sukuk to fund the Matarbari power project.

Think of it as a 'Shariah-compliant bond' where you own a piece of a project instead of just lending money.

Trade Deficit

The difference when a country buys more from other countries than it sells to them.

The $116 oil price makes our imports much more expensive, widening this gap.

It's the national version of spending more than you earn.
