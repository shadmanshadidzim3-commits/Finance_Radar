Today's lesson

Concept

The Balance of Trade

The Balance of Trade is the difference between a country's exports and its imports. If you sell more than you buy, you have a 'surplus' and your reserves grow. If you buy more than you sell—as Bangladesh is doing now with a $7.47 billion deficit—you have a 'trade deficit.'

To pay for this deficit, the country must use its savings (foreign exchange reserves) or borrow money from abroad. When reserves drop too low, the value of the local currency (the taka) usually falls because there aren't enough dollars to go around. This is why the trade deficit is the 'root cause' of the current taka depreciation.

Test yourselfIf Bangladesh's RMG exports grow by 10% but its fuel import bill grows by 50%, what happens to the trade deficit? (Answer: It widens, assuming fuel is a significant portion of imports).
