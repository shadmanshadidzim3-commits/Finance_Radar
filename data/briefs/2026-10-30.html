Today's lesson

Concept

Preferential Tariff Access

International trade operates under tariff schedules — taxes imposed on goods when they cross customs borders. Under World Trade Organization rules, most countries must charge the exact same tariffs to every trading partner. However, an exception called the Generalized System of Preferences (GSP) allows wealthy nations to lower or eliminate import taxes on goods from Least Developed Countries (LDCs).

For Bangladesh, this means European buyers pay zero duty on imported shirts, compared to a standard 12% tariff levied on non-exempt competitors. If Bangladesh graduates from LDC status without a trade pact, European retailers instantly face that 12% border tax, making Bangladeshi garments more expensive overnight. The three-year deferment preserves that 12% price advantage through 2029, protecting export volumes while domestic industries work to raise productivity.

LDC graduation deferment

A formal postponement granted by the United Nations allowing a developing nation to keep trade perks and duty-free export access reserved for the poorest countries.

The UN General Assembly approved Bangladesh's request to delay its LDC graduation by three years to 2029.

It is an extension on an academic scholarship: you keep the financial aid, but you still have to pass the finals before the clock runs out.

Imported inflation

Price increases inside a country caused by the rising cost of foreign goods and raw materials, worsened when the domestic currency loses value.

Bangladesh Bank explicitly reported that imported products are now the primary driver behind stubborn domestic consumer inflation.

When the taka falls or world oil rises, you import other countries' price tags.

Letter of credit (LC)

A formal financial guarantee issued by an importer's bank promising to pay an overseas seller once shipping documents prove the goods were sent.

Commercial banks faced intense dollar demand yesterday as letters of credit for oil and gas imports matured for cash settlement.

The commercial bank's escrow promise that makes international trade possible.

Non-performing loan (NPL)

A bank loan where the borrower has stopped making scheduled interest or principal repayments for at least 90 days.
