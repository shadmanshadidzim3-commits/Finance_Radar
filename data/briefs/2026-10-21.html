Today's lesson

Concept

The fiscal transmission of imported commodity shocks

When an economy imports essential commodities like petroleum and natural gas, price spikes on global exchanges hit national finances through two distinct channels. First, the trade balance deteriorates as commercial banks must furnish more foreign exchange to settle import letters of credit. Second, the government faces a harsh choice: either raise domestic utility tariffs or absorb massive fiscal subsidies.

If tariffs are raised, industrial companies face cost inflation and their operating margins shrink, lowering corporate tax payments. If subsidies are paid, the sovereign fiscal deficit expands, compelling the state to borrow from commercial banks. Today's 63% surge in fuel import letters of credit and Linde's compressed earnings illustrate this double hit.
