Today's lesson

Concept

The Balance of Trade

Imagine a shopkeeper who buys Tk 130 worth of goods to sell but only manages to sell Tk 100 worth of products to customers. That Tk 30 gap is the trade deficit. For a country, this gap must be filled by using savings (reserves), borrowing from abroad, or receiving gifts (remittances).

Bangladesh is currently seeing its fuel bill double, which means we are sending twice as much money abroad for the same amount of energy. This drains our dollar 'savings account' and makes the taka weaker. If we cannot sell more shirts (exports) or get more money from workers abroad (remittances), we have to keep dipping into our reserves.

Test yourselfIf a country's exports rise by 10% but its import bill for oil rises by 50%, what happens to the trade deficit if oil is a large part of its imports? (Answer: The deficit widens).

Jargon decoder

Trade deficit

When a country buys more from abroad than it sells. It matters because it creates a constant demand for foreign currency.

Our deficit jumped 31% because fuel costs doubled, making it harder to find dollars.

Think of it as a household spending more than its salary every month.

Authorized capital

The maximum amount of share capital a company is legally allowed to issue to investors.

Islami Bank is raising its limit to Tk 10,000 crore so it can eventually bring in more cash to fix its balance sheet.

It is the size of the bucket; paid-up capital is how much water is actually in it.

ACU (Asian Clearing Union)

A regional payment system where central banks settle trade bills with each other every two months.

A large ACU payment was the main reason reserves dropped to $34.85 billion today.

It is like a credit card bill that the country has to pay in one big lump every 60 days.

Tariff undercutting

Charging a price lower than the minimum rate set by the government regulator.

Two insurance giants were suspended for doing this, which risks making the whole sector unstable.

It is a 'race to the bottom' that leaves companies without enough money to pay for accidents.
