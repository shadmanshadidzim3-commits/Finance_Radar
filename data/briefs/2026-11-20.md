## Top 10 stories

**1. Capital shortfall at 22 Bangladeshi banks exceeds Tk 3 lakh crore as of June.**

According to The Business Standard, this massive capital shortfall—the gap between a bank's actual capital and what regulators require for safety—erodes lending capacity. When banks lack capital, they cannot absorb losses from non-performing loans, which are loans that borrowers have stopped repaying. This systemic weakness threatens the stability of the entire financial sector.

**2. Bangladesh Bank plans to move to a fully floating exchange rate within one month.**

According to The Business Standard, shifting to a floating exchange rate means the central bank will stop setting a fixed price for the taka. This is a key IMF condition to help stabilize foreign exchange reserves, which are the country's holdings of foreign currencies. While it may cause short-term volatility, it aims to eliminate the gap between official and informal market rates.

**3. Garment sector unrest causes daily losses of Tk 2,200 crore, says BGBA.**

The Bangladesh Garment Buy House Association (BGBA) reports that ongoing labor unrest is crippling the Readymade Garment (RMG) sector. This sector provides over 80% of export earnings, so daily losses of this magnitude directly impact the current account balance—the record of the country's transactions with the rest of the world. Prolonged unrest risks permanent order shifts to regional competitors like India or Vietnam.

The World Bank highlights that the tax-to-GDP ratio—the total tax revenue as a percentage of the economy's size—was only 6.8% in FY25. This is one of the lowest in the world.
